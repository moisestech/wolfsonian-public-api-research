The collection and the artwork

The objects are from The Wolfsonian–FIU. The questions, conversations, and juxtapositions are an artist-authored encounter. They are not a museum endorsement, a catalog match, or a record that the objects met.

Artist-authored archetypes

Family, Worker, Futurist, Archivist, and the other perspectives are artist-authored archetypes. A name such as Provenance, Promise, or At home is the stance that archetype takes. Each one is a fictional reader. None of them is a historical person, a staff member, or the museum speaking for itself.

A conversation between two objects

Each room places two archival objects into an imagined exchange. The connection shown beneath its question explains why those particular records were brought together: a shared catalog tag or an artist-proposed comparison. Neither establishes historical contact or influence.

How conversations are written

Each room starts with two collection objects and a question. Some pairs have a deliberately written dialogue; others remain research questions without an edited conversation. These are fictional interpretations, not historical quotations or live autonomous AI.

Authored scene

A script written for a specific pair of objects, with different positions and a question worth testing. Some scenes are AI-assisted editorial drafts; those are labeled in the room. Changing the selected perspective does not rewrite an authored scene.

Shared motif

A tag or symbol in both records can suggest a conversation. It does not establish that the objects, makers, or audiences met.

Imagined comparison

An artist-proposed pairing that examines a tension or possibility without claiming historical influence or shared provenance. Some proposed links remain deliberately uncertain.

For example, linking Paul Robeson to a hillside architectural fantasy through “Trees” tests a weak match. The question belongs to the artwork; the decision to keep or withdraw a network edge belongs to the editorial process.

Choosing another pair changes the question and the records under discussion. For a specific conversation, Why these two? reveals the reason for the pairing, while Record opens the source details. Interpretation is not evidence.

Playback reads the displayed script. The device voice is the default; ElevenLabs receives only the displayed words, and only after approval.
