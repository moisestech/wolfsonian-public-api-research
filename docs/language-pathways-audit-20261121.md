# Language, listener interactions and pathways audit

November 21, 2026 • Review branch, not a main-branch release

## What changed

All 40 registered scripts were reviewed against the agreed language direction. Thirty now have revised text: 16 metadata-gated conversations and 14 curatorial pathways. Ten drafts were retained because their concrete language and exchange already served the pair. Revision is not a claim of source verification, successful spoken performance or release approval.

The new work adds 22 revisions beyond the initial eight. Objects keep their materials, purposes and historical setting. The tonal range includes dry object wit, warm banter, occasional absurdity and serious passages, chosen by scene. Contemporary comparisons remain in listener questions.

## The interaction gaps

Before this pass, the visible listener repeated the same hook regardless of the room; selecting a perspective on a pathway did not reach the pathway’s voice brief; and all 20 pathways returned an empty follow-up. The pathway chooser also described listener perspectives as voices. These gaps made the voices feel interchangeable even after object dialogue was improved.

Each room now has a listener question anchored to the named pair and the scene’s focus. An optional “A question from the present” section carries the contemporary analogy. Another optional section offers a competing perspective’s question. These are interpretive questions, not autonomous agents talking or newly verified evidence. Changing the listener changes this reading, while the authored object transcript remains fixed.

Listener reflections remain outside the fixed object transcript and its audio timing. They do not create additional edges. The pathway renderer now passes the selected perspective into the scene rather than defaulting silently to Family.

## Pathways as continued inquiry

Every pathway now has a distinct follow-up that points to an object, word, material detail or unresolved question. Every pathway also has onward choices. A continuation must share an exact object accession with the current pathway; it is a reading route, not evidence of historical contact between the other objects.

Choices offer a route through each side of the pair where available, then additional alternatives, with up to three visible choices. The visitor can retain the selected perspective while moving to another pairing. The interface now identifies perspectives as listening and uses a shorter invitation to explore the questions.

Example: after the program and Futurama booklet ask when a promise becomes a souvenir, follow the program toward the fair poster or the booklet toward the television. The question changes with the partner. Those routes come from already authored pairs; this pass adds no new object edges.

## Data and coverage

The working scope remains 314 accessions, with 40 existing distinct scripted pairs across 31 objects. The public preview remains 30 objects and 36 source-eligible scripted pairs. Three registered metadata scripts remain excluded from the public count because the public subset does not supply their required tags. The source requirements were preserved.

The working data has no duplicate normalized accessions. All entries have supplied date context, which is not the same as independently verified dates. Fourteen lack object-type context and fifteen lack usable theme or motif tags. Untagged public packets may still support explicit authored comparisons. No type, tag, accent or biography was invented to fill a gap.

The audit now reports missing context, tag gaps, duplicate identities, scene word counts, record-centric openings, revision status, pathway follow-ups and dead ends. These are review signals, not automatic literary scores. Three retained scenes still use a title or record opening where that wording is part of the actual rhetorical question; those lines remain candidates for read-aloud review.

## Validation and next review

177 non-browser tests pass, including public assembly and exact-source gate checks. All eleven perspectives were checked across all twenty pathways: the object transcript and episode identity stay fixed, the listener identity changes, and modern questions remain outside object audio. Continuation checks reject self-loops and require actual shared accessions.

Browser smoke remains blocked by the host’s Chromium socket permission. Mobile appearance, the expandable listener sections and spoken rhythm still need visual and read-aloud review. This pass remains in draft PR #97; it has not been merged.

Next, review a batch containing a playful commercial pair, a serious political pair, a domestic-scale pathway and a source-boundary challenge. Judge whether the listener’s second question opens the object rather than simply restating a moral. Revise the wording and pacing before generating audio. Expand the provisional coverage queue only after that review.
