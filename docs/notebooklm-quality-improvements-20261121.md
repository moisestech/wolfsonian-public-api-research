# Improving the next listening batch

The next improvement is to give each audio discussion a particular argument and use source research where it unlocks the most existing conversations. This is a production plan, not a claim that audio has been generated or evaluated.

## First five: distinct arguments

Each pilot now has its own tension, opening, turn, ending and delivery direction. Use the host-focus prompt below for that pair, with its own verified public sources. The earlier Drive briefs remain v2 snapshots; these updated prompts are the current branch version.

### When does a household become an audience?

Tension: Receiving more information versus having the ability to answer back.

Opening: Begin with the attraction of receiving pictures as well as sound.

Turning point: Challenge whether richer reception gives the audience more agency; reconsider what it means to answer.

Ending: Leave the listener considering what a receiver cannot tell us about its audience.

Delivery: Warm banter with one dry interruption; no invented household or broadcast.

## Listen before scaling

Start with the five public-source pilots. Record actual seconds, the first useful disagreement, any unsupported factual sentence and the moment an assumption changes. Assess source correctness independently from rhythm and enjoyment. Compare the draft and a revised take under the same listening conditions. Keep the NotebookLM research hosts distinct from an exact performance of the object script.

The full tonal range remains available, selected by the scene. Modern comparisons belong to listener perspectives. No generic catchphrase or prescribed joke is required.
