# Conversation analytics and NotebookLM production index

## The current picture

The working collection contains 314 planning identities, including two registry-only research candidates. Forty existing scripted pairs cover 31 speaking objects, or 9.9% of that planning roster. There are 11 separate listener perspectives; their alternative readings do not multiply the edge count. The smaller public preview has 30 objects and 36 source-eligible scripted pairs.

Thirty scripts have revised language and ten were retained during the latest audit. The 40 scripts contain 2,558 words in total, with a median of 61 words and a range of 49–93 words per script. These are transcript counts, not measured audio lengths.

Structure is still concentrated: 32 scripts have three turns (80%), six have five (15%) and two have six (5%). Shortness is useful, but a three-turn scene can become a sequence of positions rather than a response. Listen for whether the objection actually changes the next line before producing the whole series.

The most connected object is On Munitions: Skilled Work with seven conversations, followed by 1939 A.D with six. Together they occur in twelve of the forty pairs. The Paul Robeson image and aircraft image each have one partner. Future expansion should give underserved objects additional strong partners rather than only adding to established hubs.

## Source readiness

Ten pairs have public research packets for both exact objects, with museum-published source URLs. Thirty pairs remain on primary-source hold. Public-source packet availability is not editorial, institutional or publication approval. The hold applies to missing sources, not to the creative value of the question.

Forty standalone v2 scene briefs are linked below. Each contains its two identities, the question, available source URLs, missing source records, format, short-length direction, a paste-ready host-focus prompt, evidence boundary, export name and listening-review fields. A hold brief is a planning document until its public primary sources are inspected. Do not upload a held brief merely because a draft exists.

The earlier five-scene launchpad and source briefs remain as historical working materials. The new v2 briefs reflect the revised language direction and are indexed by stable scene IDs. A display sequence is only a production order; it is not a new conversational identity.

## What the NotebookLM version is

Make one separate notebook per conversation, adding only its own authored brief and its listed museum-published sources. Do not add this master index, another conversation, the staff matrix, private Lakehouse captures, restricted photographs or the complete fixed object script.

For two-host versions, use Deep Dive or Debate and choose English / Shorter where offered. The Brief format is a single-speaker overview under two minutes, so it is a separate optional format rather than the two-host conversation. These settings are described in Google’s current generation help.

The hosts are present-day research listeners, not historical objects or museum staff. Object-specific stakes can guide their exchange. A modern analogy belongs to a host’s listener question and needs a supplied source if it makes a factual claim. The tonal palette includes dry wit, warmth, occasional absurdity and serious passages chosen to fit the material.

Target 45–90 seconds as an editorial goal, not a timing guarantee. Generated overviews may need trimming. Forty finished pieces at that target would total 30–60 minutes; the initial ten would total 7.5–15 minutes. Those are planning ranges, not recorded output.

Then refine the prompt and produce the remaining five public-source pairs. Resolve held source packs in parallel; do not fill the queue with invented facts.

## Analytics after the first exports

The production registry starts with output fields unverified. It does not assert that the user has generated zero recordings. For each take, record notebook URL, source set and version, output filename, actual duration, first useful exchange time, unsupported claims with timestamps, edit time, and review outcome. Record listening feedback separately from source correctness.

Use a factual pass/fail check plus reader ratings for clarity, specificity, rhythm and vitality. A fluent unsupported claim fails even if the take is enjoyable. Preserve both the raw take and the edited version. Only a reviewed export should enter the public app; keep the NotebookLM commentators distinct from the exact object-voice performance and its timing.

No measured listener retention, completion rate, actual clip durations or subjective listening scores are inferred here. The verified analytics in this document describe the corpus and production readiness. No NotebookLM notebook or audio output was created by this preparation step.
