# A conversation for every object

Production system, all eleven perspectives and coverage plan • November 21, 2026

The project makes inherited futures available for argument through objects, their records and the visitor’s questions. Its language should make a viewer look again at a specific object. The record must remain able to complicate the story. Personality, humor and surprise serve that encounter.

The full working roster is 314 distinct accessions: the 300-object matrix plus eight public-packet objects and six World’s Fairs research candidates. Two of those six are registry-only research candidates, not active graph nodes. The original 31-object study is a bespoke first pass. The additional 283 entries now have provisional record briefs; they still need individual authorship and inspection. All eleven interpretive perspectives have distinct language directions.

A useful stronger target is at least two different partners per object, reducing dead ends and repeated hubs. A high edge count around a few popular objects does not satisfy universal coverage. Do not make a weak pair merely to meet the number.

## 2. The editorial unit

Each object gets a stable voice card: exact identity, source context, speaking purpose, preferred vocabulary, sentence rhythm, favored conversational move, blind spot, sample line and reviewed period-phrase bank. A new object inherits only a tentative form-based direction; that is a writing brief, not a finished character. Source context such as date and place is not proof of historical dialect.

Each edge gets its own brief: exact pair, why these two, the specific difference at stake, one source-backed detail per object where available, the evidence limits, dramatic shape, claim ledger, next viewing action, draft script and review status. The question should make a visitor want to listen before they know the catalog titles.

Each perspective gets a recognizable way of questioning. Objects retain their voices when the lens changes. The perspectives remain listeners and interpretive frames in the existing interaction; these directions also govern any authored interventions. This update does not turn all eleven into simultaneous speakers.

## 3. Make the writing compelling

Start with friction that belongs to the pair: a claim someone wants to defend, an awkward detail, an interruption, a seductive promise or a practical inconvenience. Avoid introductions that merely announce the accession, title or shared tag. The interface can carry identity and the source panel can carry the evidence.

Give each line a job. It may tempt, interrupt, clarify, concede, redirect, refuse, ask for help or notice something. Change the balance of the exchange. A reply should pick up a particular word or premise from the line before it. An elegant line that could fit ten unrelated objects needs revision.

Vary the dramatic shape across batches: a disagreement with a concession; a practical negotiation; a misunderstanding corrected; an attractive promise tested; a change of scale; a quiet recognition; a refusal that remains unresolved. Objects may discover agreement as well as conflict. The default critical stance should allow delight, attraction and uncertainty.

Use ordinary conversational language: contractions, incomplete starts, short replies, an occasional aside and different sentence lengths. Let dry humor come from the object’s function or mismatch of scale. Avoid a universal 1930s patter, a mandatory catchphrase, mock accents and jokes assigned to identity groups.

Period flavor can come from a technical term, trade vocabulary, a publicity register, a translated inscription or an attested colloquialism. Use the reviewed phrase bank. Until its context and locator are checked, a phrase stays a research candidate. Plain authored speech is the default. A contemporary analogy belongs to an explicitly framed interpretation, not a fabricated historical memory.

End in different ways: point to a material detail, invite a new partner, leave a specific objection alive, ask the viewer to inspect a word, or pause on a changed understanding. Do not force every ending to ask the same question about evidence. Source inspection remains available even when it is not the last spoken line.

## 4. A repeatable batch of four

Choose four pairs that cover underserved objects and different kinds of tension. Inspect each item’s identity and one useful source passage or visual detail. Write the factual claim ledger before the script. A shared tag licenses a comparison, not a claim about shared history.

Draft roughly 3–9 turns and 60–140 spoken words when the material supports it. These are rehearsal ranges, not quotas. Choose a different dramatic shape for each pair. Preserve the object cards while writing responses that actually change the exchange.

Edit once for specificity and once for speech. Try removing the names: can the two speakers still be distinguished? Swap in an unrelated object: does the scene collapse? Cut redundant abstractions, repeated caveats and explanations already visible in the interface. Test a question-first opening with a cold reader.

Read aloud, then review on a phone with transcript and source access. Revise awkward clauses and ambiguous pronouns. Record whether the scene made the reader curious about the actual object. Approve text before generating reusable audio; audio should not conceal a weak script.

Use honest stages: record brief → inspected source brief → voice direction draft → scene draft → source checked → read aloud → artist reviewed → release candidate. Keep institutional, image and audio permissions separate where relevant. Automation prepares the roster, suggests candidates, checks structure and reports coverage. It cannot certify historical truth, conversational quality or approval.

Manual review asks six questions: Is each factual claim traceable? Are these particular objects indispensable? Do they respond to each other? Can the voices be distinguished? Is it easy to hear and read? Does it open a worthwhile way to inspect or compare? An unsupported factual claim blocks promotion. A score may organize feedback, but cannot override a failed source check.

## 5. Coverage queue and priorities

A deterministic first-pass plan suggests 282 metadata-based candidate pairs and six explicit research comparisons: 288 proposed pairs, arranged for 72 batches of four. Combined with 40 existing scripts, the projection is 328 unique pairs with two neighbors for all 314 objects. This is a planning projection, not 328 completed conversations. Weak candidates must be rejected or replaced after inspection; the order and number will change.

The metadata planner gives priority to uncovered objects, rare shared tags and balanced degree. It ignores “Unlabeled” and “Unknown.” The three untagged research gaps are handled by explicit comparison proposals, never by inventing tag matches. They are Recco City Hall, the Pennsylvania Railroad fair poster and the 1964–65 Futurama pamphlet. The latter two remain research-only until their exact records and proposed use are reviewed.

Six proposed research comparisons: Recco City Hall ↔ House on a steep hillside; Recco City Hall ↔ Trylon and Perisphere; Direct Route to the Fair ↔ Harlem invitation; Direct Route to the Fair ↔ Your World of Tomorrow; 1964–65 Futurama pamphlet ↔ 1939 Futurama book spread; 1964–65 Futurama pamphlet ↔ 1940 Futurama booklet. These are questions for source inspection, not historical links or newly activated edges.

First, evaluate the eight rewritten pilot scenes in the review branch. Then revise the remaining existing scenes in varied batches; the public preview already has enough pairs for its object count. Expand from the underserved matrix objects next. Keep one rolling batch ready for review instead of generating the full queue as finished dialogue in one pass.

## 8. Reuse and handoff

The object directions and perspective voices live in conversation-voices.mjs and object-voice-directions.mjs. The runtime supplies voice briefs alongside scenes; perspective cards now include purpose, rhythm and voice limits. The separate editorial audit counts only distinct source-eligible pairs, proposes coverage batches and reports unfilled coverage.

Run npm run editorial:audit for the public roster. Use -- --scope=matrix or -- --scope=working for broader local planning. The working roster and candidate queue are not included in the public build. New profiles can be drafted from supplied records without changing the public graph or publishing the underlying working data.

The All object voice briefs tab contains the complete working directory. Read its status column literally: authored direction drafts are distinct from provisional record briefs. Historical phrase banks remain empty in the runtime until their phrase records are checked. The first tab preserves the earlier 31-object study and its sample scenes as the original creative reference.

## Perspective directions

| Perspective | Purpose | Rhythm | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| family | Follow the practical consequences for daily life and care. | Warm, direct questions; notice the task someone still has to do. | Who has to make that work at home? |
| tourist | Test the invitation against the journey it asks someone to make. | Curious, spatial and alert to an inconvenient detour. | Lovely view. How do I actually get there? |
| novice | Ask the clear question that an expert has skipped. | Plain words, specific observations; confident curiosity. | Wait. What am I meant to notice first? |
| connoisseur | Make a formal detail change the interpretation. | Precise and sensuous; one material or compositional detail at a time. | Look at that edge. What does it make us overlook? |
| worker | Follow the task, time, maintenance and allocation of credit. | Practical verbs; a concrete follow-up rather than an automatic denunciation. | Fine. Who keeps it working? |
| archivist | Identify the particular claim that a source could confirm or change. | Brief, curious precision; intervene at the factual hinge. | Which part can we check? |
| futurist | Make the attraction of a proposed future understandable, then test it. | Energetic possibilities; enthusiasm capable of revision. | Suppose it worked. What would have to change first? |
| mourner | Notice the absence, survival or loss an attractive account leaves aside. | Unhurried and particular; allow a pause or an unfinished thought. | What had to disappear for this picture to look complete? |
| propagandist | Expose how a message recruits belief by demonstrating its rhetoric. | Sharp about a pronoun, promise, repetition or call to action. | Listen to the word “we.” Who gets included? |
| counterfeit | Offer an explicitly hypothetical inference and show where it fails. | Quick, plausible, then self-correcting; never leave the invention standing. | That would be a neat story. What would prove it wrong? |
| museum | Consider how a public account can hold an unresolved disagreement. | Hospitable, deliberate and willing to revise a label. | What could we put beside this so someone can disagree? |
